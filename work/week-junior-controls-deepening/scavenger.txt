Have a scavenger hunt
T
he long days of summer are a great time to host 
an outdoor scavenger hunt. In this game, players 
try to find specific items within a certain time limit. 
You’ll want to ask a parent for permission before you 
get started. Maybe they’ll want to do the scavenger 
hunt with you! Here’s how you can organize one for 
your family or a group of friends. 
Set the ground rules 
First, establish the boundaries for 
where you’ll have your scavenger 
hunt, whether in a backyard, on 
your street, or at a nature trail, 
park, or playground. (For more 
location ideas, see the box at right.) 
Plan the hunt during daylight hours 
and determine how long it will last, 
such as 30 or 60 minutes. Make sure 
everyone stays safe by pairing up in teams of two  
or more, and have adult chaperones present. 
Make a list
Think about items you’re likely to see and make  
a list. (You can ask friends to help you brainstorm 
possibilities.) For example, you could write down a 
specific kind of plant or flower that might be found 
growing in your neighborhood, such as a sunflower. 
Other items for your scavenger hunt could be a Little 
Free Library, a red car, a spider web, wind chimes,  
a ladybug, and a bird’s nest. A more challenging 
option: Instead of creating a list of things to find, 
write down a clue for each item without revealing  
the answer. For example, one clue could be  
“I stand on the street, lighting the way.” 
(Answer: a lamppost.) 
Start the game
Once you’ve created the list of 
items to find, give a copy of it to 
each team. Hand out pencils or 
pens so participants can check off 
items as they locate them. If everyone 
has access to a camera, they can also 
take photos of their finds. Pick a 
meeting place where everyone can gather at the 
end of the hunt. Set your timer and go!  
Distribute prizes
After the time is up, compare results to see how 
many items each team found. Hand out small prizes 
like pencils, candy, or bouncy balls to the winners. Be 
sure to take a group photo to remember the day!
Build teamwork—and create fun summer memories—with this classic challenge. 
These destinations could be fun 
locations for a scavenger hunt! 
Beach Select a designated 
area—such as between two 
lifeguard chairs—and plan to 
look for things like a shell, a 
seagull, or a sandcastle.
City Before your visit, research 
a city’s top landmarks. Add 
them to your checklist, along 
with other typical sights, such 
as a statue, a fountain, or a 
double-decker tour bus. 
Zoo Set a goal of checking off 
as many animals as you can 
find in one exhibit or in a day. If 
the zoo features a map in its 
app, you can use it as your 
scavenger hunt map.
