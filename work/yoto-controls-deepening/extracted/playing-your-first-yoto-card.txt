
Pop a card into the slot on top of your player, and it plays - that’s the magic. The first time each card plays, your Yoto needs Wi-Fi to fetch the audio; after that, the card plays anywhere, no internet needed.





Give every card a head start (10 minutes now, zero interruptions later)
Want the first experience to be seamless? After setup, do this once:

Pop each of your cards into the player, one by one. A few seconds each is fine.

Take the last card out, and leave the player switched on, charging and online overnight.

That’s it - your player quietly downloads everything while it rests. By morning, every card plays instantly, with or without Wi-Fi.


How Yoto Cards actually work
Each card holds a little chip (NFC - the same tech as contactless payment) that acts like a key. Insert the card, and your player unlocks that audio in one of two ways:

Already downloaded? It plays straight from the player - no internet needed. This is how cards your child has played before will work.

First play, or not finished downloading? The audio streams over Wi-Fi while the player downloads it in the background - so you’ll need to be online, with a decent connection. (Some cards, like radio stations, always stream.)
Curious which is happening? Your player’s display tells you - See: What the icons on your Yoto’s display mean.


If the audio stutters or breaks up
Almost always one of two things, and both pass:

Busy Wi-Fi or a weak signal - move the player closer to the router. 

Lots of downloads at once - typical on day one when every card is new. Give it time (or run the overnight routine above).


If a card won’t play at all 

Take it out, and try again.

Try another Yoto Card. If other cards play normally, test the affected card using a phone with NFC:

Make sure NFC is switched on, if your phone has this setting.

Hold the card against your phone’s NFC reader-usually near the top or on the back-for a few seconds.

If nothing happens, remove your phone case and try moving the card slowly across the back of your phone.

If possible, scan a Yoto Card that works normally for comparison.




Check the display. A red stop sign means the card wasn’t recognised.
